## Hallmark study diagnosis — primary artifac

REFERENCE
User-owned `impeccable-whole-site-redesign-v3` preview, studied as the single
primary artifact before the v4.1 redesign.

WHAT TO LEARN
The v3 artifact already owned a credible institutional palette, local type
roles, route and custody language, complete workflow coverage, and a stable
public/authenticated split. Its dominant fingerprint was a conventional
Workbench: persistent left rail, top command row, page heading, then a repeated
sequence of bordered panels and equal-weight cards. Public entry was a portal
tutorial rather than an institutional introduction. The motion language was
small and productive, but the toggle, menu, back control, and loading treatmen
did not yet carry the visual world's identity.

WHAT TO ADAPT
Carry the oxblood/gold identity, Newsreader wordmark, Bricolage display role,
IBM Plex Sans operational text, route/custody semantics, warm-light and
oxblood-tinted-dark grounds, full route registry, and finite motion discipline.
Change macrostructure from Workbench to an editorial Route Map: institutional
public gateway, asymmetric operational brief, decision workbench, route-coded
navigation banks, and a search-led command surface. Use N13 authenticated
command navigation, N5 floating public navigation, and a statement close.

WHAT NOT TO COPY
Do not preserve the tutorial-first public landing, equal-card priority wall,
generic compact toggle, inherited hamburger/back geometry, decorative corner
rules without meaning, or repeated containers that group content spacing could
already explain.

WHERE IT APPLIES IN HAU-USC LOGISTICS
The public hero and portal hierarchy, authenticated overview, command search,
route rail, priority system, loading states, theme/menu/back controls, and
mobile navigation. Confidence is high because both the v3 source artifact and
its identical-viewport captures were available locally.

## Reference records

REFERENCE
[Tabnav — Best Website Design Examples](https://tabnav.com/blog/best-website-design-examples)

WHAT TO LEARN
Editorial confidence comes from decisive hierarchy, legible type, purposeful
small motion, and composition that changes with the content.

WHAT TO ADAPT
Use asymmetric emphasis and generous negative space instead of equal-card
grids; let one operational priority lead.

WHAT NOT TO COPY
Do not copy showcase layouts, imagery, brand treatments, or decorative effects.

WHERE IT APPLIES IN HAU-USC LOGISTICS
Public landing hierarchy, authenticated overview, and priority surfaces.

REFERENCE
[BlueZoo — Web Design Inspiration](https://bluezooweb.com/best-websites-for-web-design-inspiration-brilliant-examples-to-boost-your-creativity/)

WHAT TO LEARN
Distinctive composition can coexist with familiar navigation and clear utility;
real sites should be studied, synthesized, tested, and never cloned.

WHAT TO ADAPT
Use a bold focal region, controlled glass, and interaction detail while keeping
high-frequency operational controls predictable.

WHAT NOT TO COPY
Do not import agency-showcase effects, client-specific motifs, or novelty tha
competes with work.

WHERE IT APPLIES IN HAU-USC LOGISTICS
Campus-image landing, command surface, and localized theme control.

REFERENCE
[Hallmark](https://github.com/nutlope/hallmark)

WHAT TO LEARN
Macrostructure precedes styling. Study extracts transferable DNA, audit exposes
template fingerprints, and redesign must replace the fingerprint while
preserving information architecture and behavior.

WHAT TO ADAPT
Change the public portal from tutorial cards to an institutional gateway and
the overview from a panel sequence to a command brief plus workbench.

WHAT NOT TO COPY
Do not import Hallmark demo themes, layouts, assets, or decorative signatures.

WHERE IT APPLIES IN HAU-USC LOGISTICS
Whole shell, public gateway, overview, queue/detail hierarchy, and anti-slop
review.

REFERENCE
[Impeccable](https://github.com/pbakaus/impeccable)

WHAT TO LEARN
Focused critique, hierarchy, layout, type, color, motion, adaptation, hardening,
audit, and polish passes produce better evidence than a single vague redesign
instruction.

WHAT TO ADAPT
Use a distinct pass for each concern and record command, target, finding, and
implemented change.

WHAT NOT TO COPY
Do not copy its demonstration visual world or treat command invocation as proof
of quality.

WHERE IT APPLIES IN HAU-USC LOGISTICS
Controls, typography, light/dark composition, responsive reflow, edge states,
and final verification.

REFERENCE
[Laws of UX](https://lawsofux.com/), including
[Fitts’s Law](https://lawsofux.com/fittss-law/),
[Hick’s Law](https://lawsofux.com/hicks-law/),
[Proximity](https://lawsofux.com/law-of-proximity/),
[Uniform Connectedness](https://lawsofux.com/law-of-uniform-connectedness/), and
[Von Restorff Effect](https://lawsofux.com/von-restorff-effect/)

WHAT TO LEARN
Large targets reduce acquisition error; grouped choices reduce decision load;
proximity forms groups before borders do; visible connections must represen
real relationships; rare emphasis makes priority legible.

WHAT TO ADAPT
Keep 44px targets, group by spacing, reserve lifecycle lines for real custody
relationships, and give only the urgent signal exceptional weight.

WHAT NOT TO COPY
Do not use UX laws to hide necessary complexity, create dark patterns, or make
color the only priority cue.

WHERE IT APPLIES IN HAU-USC LOGISTICS
Theme/menu/back controls, portal actions, filters, attention signals, lifecycle
states, and mobile navigation.

REFERENCE
[Nielsen Norman Group — 5 Visual-design Principles in UX](https://media.nngroup.com/media/articles/attachments/Principles_Visual_Design-A4.pdf)

WHAT TO LEARN
Relative scale signals rank; hierarchy determines reading order; balance can be
asymmetric; contrast communicates difference; Gestalt grouping creates a
coherent whole.

WHAT TO ADAPT
Use two or three strong size ranks, an asymmetric but balanced overview, clear
navigation/content contrast, and alignment/proximity before containers.

WHAT NOT TO COPY
Do not convert the examples into a rigid token recipe or rely on bright color
alone for importance.

WHERE IT APPLIES IN HAU-USC LOGISTICS
Overview, hero, Request Center, inventory hierarchy, and queue/detail reading
order.

REFERENCE
[Material Design — Material Motion](https://m1.material.io/motion/material-motion.html)

WHAT TO LEARN
Motion should explain organization, spatial relationship, cause/effect, and
guided focus. Small changes should feel quick; large spatial changes may take
longer but remain coherent.

WHAT TO ADAPT
Use the owner’s 100–320ms control budgets, finite route and overlay transitions,
contextual skeletons, linear progress, and one focal motion thesis.

WHAT NOT TO COPY
Do not import Material component geometry, Android styling, ink ripples, long
stagger chains, crossing paths, or spinner-first loading.

WHERE IT APPLIES IN HAU-USC LOGISTICS
Celestial plate, menu transformation, back travel, overlays, route continuity,
and loading states.

REFERENCE
[web.dev — prefers-reduced-motion](https://web.dev/articles/prefers-reduced-motion)

WHAT TO LEARN
Reduced motion may require redesigning the animated experience, not merely
slowing it. CSS and JavaScript paths must preserve a clear state withou
unnecessary travel.

WHAT TO ADAPT
Keep immediate state and fast color/opacity feedback while removing travel,
rotation, stagger, and cinematic arrival.

WHAT NOT TO COPY
Do not blanket-disable feedback that makes state legible or rely on
`animationend` for application logic.

WHERE IT APPLIES IN HAU-USC LOGISTICS
Theme, menu, back, route transitions, dialogs, landing entrance, skeletons, and
notifications.

REFERENCE
[Mobbin MCP](https://mobbin.com/mcp)

WHAT TO LEARN
Its value is searchable evidence from shipped products rather than invented
patterns.

WHAT TO ADAPT
When an authenticated integration is available, compare multiple products for
queue/detail, operations tables, command search, notifications, filters, mobile
bottom navigation, theme controls, and progress patterns.

WHAT NOT TO COPY
Do not clone proprietary screens or infer specific product patterns from a
marketing page.

WHERE IT APPLIES IN HAU-USC LOGISTICS
Request queues, inventory, lifecycle detail, command search, notifications,
mobile navigation, and theming.

## Applied synthesis

- Premium shell: compact route identity, functional command search, asymmetric
  overview brief, and restrained depth rather than a generic card wall.
- Light/dark control: a dark celestial capsule with both endpoints visible and
  a moving active plate; programmatic state and reduced motion stay truthful.
- Operational density: dominant exception signal, table-first primary work,
  and a separate contextual intelligence rail.
- Public hierarchy: USC identity and official channel lead into production
  portal boundaries; the tutorial stepper is removed.
- Responsive navigation: persistent rail at wide sizes, kinetic drawer control
  at narrow sizes, and task-preserving bottom navigation.
- Loading: structure-preserving rows, bounded progress, truthful status text,
  and finite reveal without fake percentages.
- Anti-AI-slop: no equal three-card hero, decorative lifecycle lines, gratuitous
  glass everywhere, bounce, neon, fake metrics, or copied shipped-product UI.
